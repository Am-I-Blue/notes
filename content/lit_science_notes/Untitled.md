## Introduction
It is possible to draw a direct line between Dicks androids and homosexuality. In a heteronormativ world lesbians and gays are forced to hide their identity and if they fail to ’pass’, they are threatened with violence. Simultaneously, because sexuality cannot be scientifically measured it causes paranoia in the straight population, anyone might be one of _them_.

>Passing is further complicated by the fact that to be „unmarked“ is to occupy a
>position of privilege, in which the subject hides behind an apparent transparency.
>White individuals, for instance, are in a constant state of passing as having no ethnic
>or racial identity at all, as having „nothing to say“ about race, or as somehow existing
>outside the volatile world of „racial tensions. – (Schlossberg, p. 5)

What makes Dicks _Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?_ special is that the protagonist Rick Deckard loses his power of being unmarked. And he is dragged down to the queer power struggle
of the androids. Characters like Luba Luft accuse him of being an android himself. His roll is switched from a dominant one to fleeing the police. At the same time he's struggling with his
desire for androids despite having a human wife. Further complicated by the fact that despite their looks androids have no gender. The society of _Do Androids [...]_ values fertility very highly which puts infertile specials and androids below humans in the hierarchy.

It is worth asking what a queer reading of Philip K. Dicks Do Androids [...] uncovers about the power structure of sexuality and gender? To do that, I will reference _The dangers of individualism and the Human Relationship to Technology[...]_ by Christopher A. Sims, in which he writes, that after humanity invented the androids it desperately needed reasons why it should still be the superior species. (Sims, p. 68)

_Passing_ published by Linda Schlossberg and Maria Carla Sánchez is a collections which mainly establishes "passing" as a code(?). Rachel Carrolls _Imitations of Life[...]_ is used to understand the language behinde queere powerstrcutures, as is _Quiet Refusals[...]_ by Nima Behroozi Moghadam und Porugiv Farideh. David Hueberts _Species Panic_ compares humans and androids in regard to sexiality.
